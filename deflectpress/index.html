DeflectPress: FWD analysis for IRC:115 overlay design

DeflectPress is free FWD analysis software for Windows that turns falling weight deflectometer data into a full IRC:115-2014 structural evaluation and overlay design for flexible pavements — on any road type: NH, SH, MDR or urban.

Features

Falling weight deflectometer analysis, start to finish

Every step of the IRC:115-2014 pavement structural evaluation in one app, with the clause or equation shown next to each result.

Import FWD data and check every deflection basin

  • KUAB .fwd files, or a table pasted from Excel
  • Checks that the deflection basin falls with distance, load is 40 ± 4 kN, sensor capacity, at least 10 µm at 1.2 m, and pavement temperature ≤ 45 °C
  • Deflections normalised to 40 kN; excluded points keep their reason, and any value you correct by hand is marked, listed and can be restored

Data checks: copied FWD bowls and inner / outer lanes

  • New in v1.6: a copied-bowl check on the new Data checks page. Every deflection bowl is compared with every other; points whose deflections are all in one ratio (exact copies, or copies multiplied by one factor such as 0.875) are listed by group with serial no., lane, chainage, load, factor and section, as warnings under IRC:115 cl. 6.1.1 (obviously erroneous data must be removed). Real bowls of similar shape are not flagged (match within 0.01 µm)
  • The page also groups point warnings by reason, lists values edited by hand, and shows the bowl shape (D200/D0) for information only: IRC:115 sets no limit on it
  • New in v1.6: inner and outer lanes. Each FWD file has a lane (outer lane, inner lane, paved shoulder, or read from the file name); points show as “LHS - Inner” in the app and the reports, and test spacing is checked for each wheel path against its own IRC:115 Table 2 limit (e.g. fair, four-lane divided: 65 m outer lane, 130 m inner lane)
  • New in v1.6.1: raw drops or averaged values. Each FWD file says whether it holds the raw drops or values already averaged per point, and Paste table asks which one. For averaged data the “fewer than 3 drops” warning is not raised (the averaging was done before entry, cl. 6.1.1); raw drops are still averaged by DeflectPress and checked for 3 drops. KUAB .fwd files are always raw drops
  • New in v1.6: editable FWD data. In the Measured view you can correct chainage, load, deflections and pavement temperature; every change is marked, listed on the Data checks page and saved in the project, and “Restore point” undoes it

FWD data by lane, editable in the Measured view

  • Lane column for each FWD file (Table 2): outer lane, inner lane, paved shoulder, or auto from the file name
  • Correct a chainage, load, deflection or pavement temperature in place; edited values are marked and can be restored
  • Normalised peak deflection D0 chart for the LHS and RHS wheel paths

Homogeneous sections by cumulative difference

  • IRC:115 Eq 3 on SCI or D0, with automatic splits that keep at least 1 km and 12 points
  • Or set breaks by hand, one section per lane or group, or point-wise analysis
  • New in v1.6: every homogeneous section is checked against 1.0 km and 12 points (cl. 6.2.4; 1–2 points = localised rehabilitation) and the Eq 1 minimum sample (cl. 5.4.2), whatever minimums the auto split uses; manual breaks are flagged for comparison with the cumulative difference (Eq 3)
  • New in v1.1: manual breaks cut exactly at the chainages you type, and a skip reach (e.g. 2.3-2.6) leaves out a reconstruction or no-FWD-design reach: its end points go to the sections outside it, points inside are not used, and it is listed as “Skip reach” in the sections table and Table 9.1. Worked examples for breaks and skip reaches are shown under the table, for instance two skips: BREAKS 1.0 · SKIP 0.5-0.75, 2.3-2.6

FWD back calculation of layer moduli

  • Three-layer backcalculation with KGPBACK's objective: grid search, then Levenberg–Marquardt
  • Modulus ranges from Appendix III, including Eq III.2 for the subgrade per point
  • Fit % per point and range-limit flags; KGPBACK output can be imported
  • New in v1.4: subgrade CBR input on the Layers page (9 or 9%) for the 5–20 × CBR subgrade range (App III.8.4 ii), with a check if it is missing

Temperature and seasonal correction

  • Bituminous modulus corrected to 35 °C (Eq 4–5)
  • Seasonal correction: winter Eq 6 / Eq 9, summer Eq 7 / Eq 8
  • New in v1.2: enter the testing month and monsoon type (south-west or north-east) and the cl. 6.5 season is picked for you; you can still change it, and a warning shows if it differs from the month
  • 15th percentile design moduli for each homogeneous section
  • New in v1.6: modulus limits reported per layer, and the testing season reads “Monsoon / post-monsoon” (cl. 6.5.1)

Remaining life and IRC 115 overlay design

  • Auto overlay material: VG40 at 3000 MPa for NH / expressways or 20 msa and above, otherwise VG30 at 2000 MPa (IRC:37-2018 Tables 9.1 / 9.2)

FAQ

FWD analysis questions

How does DeflectPress do IRC:115 FWD analysis?

It follows the IRC:115-2014 workflow step by step: import the falling weight deflectometer data, check it and normalise the deflections to 40 kN, divide the road into homogeneous sections, backcalculate the layer moduli, correct them for temperature and season, take the 15th percentile design moduli, then work out remaining life and the bituminous overlay. Each step shows the clause or equation it uses.

How does FWD back calculation of layer moduli work?

DeflectPress fits a three-layer system (bituminous, granular, subgrade) to each measured deflection basin. It uses the same objective as KGPBACK, the sum of squared relative deflection errors, with a grid search followed by Levenberg–Marquardt within the IRC:115 Appendix III modulus ranges, including Eq III.2 for the subgrade. Each point gets a fit percentage, and moduli that end on a range limit are flagged. KGPBACK output files can also be imported.

How are the temperature and seasonal corrections applied?

The bituminous modulus is corrected to 35 °C with IRC:115 Eq 4–5, except where the code says not to (bituminous layer under 40 mm, Poor sections, cold or high-altitude areas). Granular and subgrade moduli get the seasonal correction: winter Eq 6 and Eq 9, summer Eq 7 and Eq 8, and none for post-monsoon testing. Warnings appear when values are outside each equation's range.

Can DeflectPress pick the seasonal correction from the FWD testing month?

Yes. On the Corrections page, choose the testing month and the monsoon type: south-west (Jun–Sep, most of India) or north-east (Oct–Dec, the south-east coast). DeflectPress then picks the IRC:115 cl. 6.5 season. By default, with the south-west monsoon, Jun–Nov gets no correction, Dec–Feb is winter (Eq 6 / Eq 9) and Mar–May is summer (Eq 7 / Eq 8); with the north-east monsoon, Oct–Feb gets no correction and Mar–Sep is summer. This month-to-season mapping is the app’s default, not an IRC table: you can still change the season by hand, and Results & Checks warns if it differs from what the month suggests. The report and Excel output show the testing month.

How is the IRC 115 overlay design worked out from FWD data?

For each homogeneous section, DeflectPress uses the 15th percentile design moduli to find the remaining life by Eq 16/17 under the standard dual wheel. It then solves for the thinnest bituminous overlay that carries the full design traffic, as IRC:115 requires; balance traffic is available as an option. The overlay material is set automatically: VG40 at 3000 MPa for NH and expressways or 20 msa and above, otherwise VG30 at 2000 MPa (IRC:37-2018 Tables 9.1 and 9.2).

How does it find homogeneous sections?

By the cumulative difference method of IRC:115 Eq 3, on SCI or D0. A section is split where the cumulative difference changes most, as long as both parts keep at least 1 km and 12 points and their means really differ. You can also set breaks by hand, use one section per lane or group, or analyse point by point.

Can I leave out a reconstruction reach from the FWD overlay design?

Yes. Enter it as a skip reach on the Sections page, for example 2.3-2.6 or 2+300 to 2+600. The test points at its two ends go to the sections outside it, points inside are not used (the reason is shown), and no overlay is designed for that reach. It is listed as “Skip reach” in the sections table and in Table 9.1, with a note to design it separately as new pavement to IRC:37. Manual section breaks also cut exactly at the chainages you type; a point on a break stays in the section before it.

Which FWD data formats can it import?

KUAB .fwd files and a table pasted from Excel (group, chainage, load, deflections D1 to Dn, pavement and air temperature). KGPBACK backcalculation output can be imported too.

Has DeflectPress been checked against the code?

Yes, against the IRC:115 Appendix IV worked example. The temperature and seasonal corrections and the 15th percentile moduli match exactly. Under the wheel centre the strains match the code: 284.4 vs 284.5 microstrain for the existing pavement, and 159.8 vs 159.8 microstrain with the 95 mm overlay. The layered-elastic solver matches IITPAVE to 0.2%.

Can DeflectPress make the FWD report in Word?

Yes. On the Word Report page, choose Format 01 or Format 02 and DeflectPress writes an editable FWD report in Word (.docx) with contents, tables, annexures and charts. Format 01 has a cover with the client and testing-agency blocks and logos, lists of tables and figures, chapters 1 and 2, and landscape Annexures 1.1 to 1.4 (deflections, moduli, remaining life, and overlay with the BC / DBM split). Format 02 has a running header and page numbers, contents, lists of tables, figures and annexures, acronyms, chapters 1 to 5 with Tables 1 to 4, and Annexures 1 (basic data) and 2 (back calculation). The contents and the table and figure lists are real Word fields, so the IRC:115 report template stays easy to edit. From v1.4 both Word reports keep the fonts, colours, sizes, spacing, headings and tables of the two report formats exactly, and fill in the project data, results and annexure rows.

Do I need survey photos for the FWD study report?

No. Survey photos are optional. If you add them, they print two per row with captions (Photo 1.1, 1.2 …). Without photos, the photo section and its list-of-figures entries are left out of the report.

Do I need KGPBACK or IITPAVE screenshots for the FWD report?

No. DeflectPress draws the back-calculation figures (input, and measured vs computed deflection bowl) and the strain-analysis figures (input and results for the existing pavement and, when needed, the overlaid pavement) from its own results. If you prefer your own KGPBACK or IITPAVE screenshots, add them on the Word Report page and they replace the drawn figures; the figures renumber automatically.

Where do I enter the subgrade CBR?

On the Layers page, as 9 or 9%. DeflectPress uses it for the 5 to 20 × CBR subgrade modulus range (IRC:115 Appendix III.8.4 ii), and a check flags a missing CBR.

When does a pavement need strengthening as per IRC:115?

When the remaining life is less than the design traffic in msa (IRC:115 cl. 8.4 ix). The remaining life is the shorter of the fatigue life of the bituminous layer (Eq 16) and the subgrade rutting life (Eq 17), worked out from the FWD-backcalculated moduli. The overlay is then sized so that both lives reach the full design traffic (cl. 8.4 x). DeflectPress shows, for every homogeneous section, the decision (No strengthening, Strengthening, or Reconstruction when even 300 mm of overlay is not enough) and the governing criterion.

Does subgrade CBR matter in FWD overlay design?

Not directly. IRC:115 backcalculates the subgrade modulus from the measured deflections. The CBR only sets the 5 to 20 × CBR search range for that modulus (IRC:115 Appendix III.8.4 ii); DeflectPress can instead use the outer deflections (Eq III.2) or a 20 to 100 MPa range.

How do I find copied or duplicated FWD deflection data?

DeflectPress compares every deflection bowl with every other and lists the points whose deflections are exact or scaled copies of another point (all sensors in one ratio, for example 0.875), on the Data checks page, with serial no., lane, chainage, load, factor and section. IRC:115 cl. 6.1.1 asks for obviously erroneous data to be removed; real bowls of similar shape are not flagged.

Does IRC:115 require inner-lane FWD testing?

On four-lane and wider roads, IRC:115 Table 2 asks for the outer wheel path of the outer lanes and of the more distressed inner lane, the inner lane at double the spacing (for a fair pavement, 65 m and 130 m). Two-lane roads are tested along both outer wheel paths. DeflectPress checks the test spacing for each lane against its own limit.

What is the minimum homogeneous section length in IRC:115?

1.0 km with at least 12 test points (IRC:115 cl. 6.2.4); a sub-section with only one or two points needs localised rehabilitation. DeflectPress also checks the Eq 1 minimum sample size (15 points for a fair section).

Can I paste FWD deflections I have already averaged?

Yes. Choose “Averaged” when pasting (or in the DATA column of the FWD file list); DeflectPress then uses the values as the point averages and does not ask for 3 drops. Paste raw drops instead and DeflectPress averages them as IRC:115 cl. 6.1.1 describes. KUAB .fwd machine files are always read as raw drops.

What does DeflectPress not cover?

Rigid pavements (IRC:117), Benkelman beam deflection (IRC:81), FWD formats other than KUAB .fwd and pasted tables, and a cemented-base fatigue criterion. It is an engineering design aid: results must be compared with distress data, test pits and cores, and the engineer is responsible for the inputs and the adopted design.
