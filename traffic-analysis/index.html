Features

Every traffic survey, analysed the IRC way

Enter only the surveys you have — each module works on its own. Raw counts are never changed silently: edits to survey data are logged and printed in the report.

Vehicle classes and PCU conversion (IRC:64 / IRC:106)

  • The 17 classes from the field sheets, editable
  • Five PCU sets: rural (IRC:64 Table 1), urban (IRC:106 Table 1, by share of traffic), intersection (SP:41 Table 3.1), roundabout (IRC:65 Table 5.2) and custom

Traffic volume count (TVC) data

  • 15-minute or hourly classified counts, pasted from Excel
  • 7-day coverage check, plus missing and duplicate periods

Traffic volume count analysis: ADT, AADT, growth and capacity

  • ADT, composition, directional split and rolling peak hour with PHF
  • Seasonal factor (none, fuel sales, IRC:108 index or direct), AADT and CVPD
  • Traffic growth rate and projection: Method 1 or Method 2, scenarios and a trend-regression helper
  • Capacity: IRC:64 design service volumes (single-lane to 8-lane), IRC:106 urban, service roads, and the year the SP:73 / SP:84 lane-addition threshold is reached

Turning movement count (TMC) analysis and junction flow diagram

  • Any number of arms: 3-arm T / Y, 4-arm cross, 5-arm and 6-arm templates; turns from the arm bearings
  • Peak hour, design-year projection and an automatic junction flow diagram
  • IRC:93 signal warrants 1 and 2; IRC:65 roundabout entry capacity, v/c, delay and level of service; SP:41 lane checks

Origin destination (OD) survey analysis

  • Zone coding with aliases and expansion factors from the counts
  • II / IE / EI / EE split (IRC:102), trip-length distribution and OD-weighted growth
  • Desire-line diagram of PCU trips

Axle load survey and VDF calculation

  • Wheel-load or axle-load entry
  • Vehicle damage factor by class and direction (IRC:37 Eq 4.1–4.4), CVPD-weighted VDF and axle load spectrum
  • Sample-size check (Table 4.1) and indicative VDF (Table 4.2)

Speed and delay study (moving observer method)

  • Journey speed and flow by the moving observer method
  • Delay causes, speed–flow fit and spot-speed percentiles

Design traffic in msa (IRC:37), straight into PavePress

  • IRC:37 Eq 4.5–4.6 with lane distribution factors
  • Exports a .pave file that opens in PavePress with the same msa

IRC checks and reports

  • Every result carries IRC checks: Fail / Check / Note, with the clause
  • PDF and HTML report following SP:19 chapter 6, CSV, and an Excel workbook with live formulas
  • Optional client and consultant logos; a built-in, anonymised sample

Checked against reference workbooks

  • ADT, VDF, the TMC peak (3010 PCU), the OD expansion factors and the seasonal factor match the reference workbooks
  • Excel export: 1,972 formula cells give the same values after Excel's full recalculation, with 0 mismatches

Engineering aid. The engineer is responsible for the survey data, the inputs and the adopted results.

FAQ

Traffic survey analysis questions

How does Traffic Analysis work out ADT, AADT and the seasonal factor?

ADT comes from the classified traffic volume count (15-minute or hourly, with 7-day coverage and checks for missing or duplicate periods). AADT is ADT corrected by a seasonal factor, which you choose following IRC:108: none, from fuel sales, from the IRC:108 index, or entered directly. Composition, directional split, the rolling peak hour with PHF and CVPD are worked out at the same time.

Which junctions can the turning movement count (TMC) analysis handle, and does it check signal warrants?

Any number of arms, with layout templates for 3-arm T and Y, 4-arm cross, 5-arm and 6-arm junctions. Turns are worked out from the arm bearings. It finds the peak hour, projects it to the design year, draws the junction flow diagram, and checks IRC:93 signal warrants 1 and 2, IRC:65 roundabout entry capacity with v/c, delay and level of service, and SP:41 lane needs.

How is the VDF calculated from an axle load survey?

Enter wheel or axle loads per vehicle. The vehicle damage factor is worked out by class and direction with IRC:37-2018 Eq 4.1 to 4.4, then weighted by CVPD. The app also builds the axle load spectrum, checks the sample size against Table 4.1, and shows the indicative VDF from Table 4.2 for comparison.

How is the design traffic in msa calculated, and can it go to PavePress?

Design traffic follows IRC:37-2018 Eq 4.5 and 4.6, using the CVPD, growth rate, design period, lane distribution factor and VDF from the surveys. It can be exported as a .pave file that opens in PavePress, the free IRC:37 pavement design app, and gives the same msa there.

How is an OD survey expanded?

Interviews are coded to zones (with aliases for spelling variants), then each sample is expanded by the factor count / samples for its station, direction and vehicle class, using the counts from the TVC. The expanded trips are split into internal–internal, internal–external, external–internal and through (external–external) traffic, with a trip-length distribution, OD-weighted growth and a desire-line diagram.

Which PCU factors are used for PCU conversion?

Five sets: rural roads (IRC:64 Table 1), urban roads (IRC:106 Table 1, by share of traffic), intersections (SP:41 Table 3.1), roundabouts (IRC:65 Table 5.2), and a custom set. The 17 vehicle classes from the field sheets can be edited.

How are the traffic growth rate and projection handled?

Method 1 uses one rate for motorised traffic and one for non-motorised traffic; Method 2 uses class-wise rates by period. Scenarios and a trend-regression helper are included. The projected traffic is checked against IRC:64 or IRC:106 capacity, giving the year when the SP:73 / SP:84 lane-addition threshold is reached.

Has Traffic Analysis been checked?

Yes. ADT, VDF, the TMC peak (3010 PCU), the OD expansion factors and the seasonal factor match the reference workbooks. In the Excel export, 1,972 formula cells give the same values after Excel's full recalculation. It is an engineering aid: the engineer is responsible for the survey data, the inputs and the adopted results.
