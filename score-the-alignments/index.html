Features

Evaluation of alignment options, the Appendix-8 way

Simple mode works like the Excel matrix: four pages, and you type the details of each alternative. Switch on the detail pages only if you want the app to work out land, costs and geometrics for you.

Alternatives and aspect weights

  • 2 to 6 alternatives — bypass alignment selection, realignments or route selection for a new road
  • Weighted score from engineering, socio-economic, environmental, cost and safety aspects (25 / 20 / 20 / 15 / 20 %), editable and checked to total 100 %
  • Add a new aspect with its own weight

IRC:SP:19-2020 Appendix-8 scoring matrix

  • Templates for the 41 Appendix-8 parameters or an extended 49-parameter bypass list
  • Rules per parameter: Lower is better, Higher is better, Graded words or Score entered, plus an “If all 0” setting
  • + Add / Remove your own parameters; paste rows from Excel

How the scoring rules work

  • Worked examples for each rule on the matrix page, and hover help on the column headers
  • Best value = 1, others proportionate; mixed terrain is length-weighted (“Plain 11, Mountainous 22”)

Alignment ranking and weight sensitivity

  • Ranking, weighted score, lead over the 2nd option and a written recommendation, with a chart
  • Weight sensitivity: equal weights and each aspect +10 points; flags a change of first place or a close result
  • Checks with clauses: weights total, missing details, forest land (cl. 18.6), ROB (cl. 18.9), canal crossings (cl. 18.11)

Optional: land acquisition cost (RFCTLARR Act 2013)

  • Survey-number or village-wise land schedule, pasted from the LA / 3A schedule
  • Multiplier 1–2, 100 % solatium and 12 % additional amount
  • Nature of land classified into the nine Appendix-8 land-use categories, feeding the matrix

Optional: structures, trees and R&R

  • Structures and trees with 100 % solatium; small houses and important buildings feed the demolition parameters
  • Displaced families and R&R entitlements from the Second Schedule

Optional: pre-construction and civil cost

  • Utility shifting, environment and other items: rate × quantity × (1 + add-on %)
  • Civil cost from the block estimate or a build-up; overall cost of each alternative

Optional: geometric design checks (IRC:73, IRC:SP:84)

  • Design speed, minimum radius and maximum gradient to IRC:73, IRC:SP:73, IRC:SP:84 or IRC:SP:87
  • Gradient and road geometry grades feed the matrix

Checked against the code and a real study

  • Reproduces the IRC:SP:19-2020 Appendix-8 worked example: weighted scores 7.14 / 7.65 / 7.30, Alternative 2 preferred
  • Matches a real bypass evaluation workbook to 1e-9 (weighted scores 8.679 / 8.694 / 9.203)
  • Excel export: the same values after Excel's full recalculation, with 0 mismatches

FAQ

Alignment option study questions

How does the IRC:SP:19 Appendix-8 scoring matrix score the alignment alternatives?

Each aspect is divided into parameters worth 1 mark each. For every parameter the best alternative scores 1 and the others a proportionate value. Lower is better (length, structures, land, cost): score = least value / value. Higher is better (use of the existing road): score = value / highest. Graded words (plain, rolling, mountainous; good, fair, poor; safe) become factors, length-weighted for mixed terrain. You can also type a score directly, and set what each parameter scores when every alternative is 0.

What are the aspect weights, and can they be changed?

The suggested weights are engineering 25 %, socio-economic 20 %, environmental 20 %, cost 15 % and safety 20 %. They can be edited; the app checks that they total 100 % and flags any change from the suggested values. A weight-sensitivity table shows the ranking with equal weights and with each aspect raised by 10 points.

Can I add my own parameter or aspect to the alignment evaluation?

Yes. Click a row of the evaluation matrix and press + Add: a new 1-mark parameter appears below it in the same aspect. Type its name and unit, pick the rule and enter the details for each alternative; the scores, ranking and reports update at once. Remove deletes the selected row. A whole new aspect can be added with its weight on the Alternatives & weights page.

Do I have to work out the land acquisition cost?

No. By default you type the details of each alternative straight into the matrix, as in the Appendix-8 table or an Excel sheet. Land acquisition under the RFCTLARR Act 2013, structures, trees and R&R, utility / environment / civil cost and geometric checks are optional detail pages you can switch on; their results fill the matrix rows you leave blank.

Can the alignment comparison be exported to Excel or PDF for a DPR?

Yes. The PDF and HTML report follows the Appendix-8 table layout, with the ranking, recommendation, chart, sensitivity and checks. The Excel workbook has live formulas for the scores, subtotals, weights, weighted score and RANK. CSV and a .sta project file are also available.

Which alternatives and parameters does it handle?

2 to 6 alternatives, for bypasses, realignments and green-field roads. Templates load the 41 parameters of the Appendix-8 matrix, or an extended 49-parameter bypass list that adds flyovers, LVUP / SVUP / VOP, underpasses, box culverts, deviation from the existing road and termination.

What geometric design checks are included?

On the optional Geometrics page, design values for IRC:73, IRC:SP:73, IRC:SP:84 or IRC:SP:87 check the design speed, the minimum radius (R = V² / 127 (e + f)) and the maximum gradient. The results give the gradient grade and the overall road geometry grade that feed the matrix. The values are editable, so confirm them against the edition you use.

Has Score the Alignments been checked?

Yes. It reproduces the IRC:SP:19-2020 Appendix-8 worked example (weighted scores 7.14 / 7.65 / 7.30, Alternative 2 preferred). A real bypass evaluation workbook is matched to 1e-9 (weighted scores 8.679 / 8.694 / 9.203), and the Excel export gives the same values after Excel's full recalculation. It is an engineering aid: the engineer is responsible for the inputs and the adopted alignment.
